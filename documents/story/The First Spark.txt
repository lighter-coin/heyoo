################ (Ordinary World) ################
Long, long ago—before meme coins, Wi-Fi, or even the concept of lunch—the world was a miserable place. Life was cold, dark, and completely unfair. The creatures of the land either had sharp teeth, terrifying speed, or both. Food was a luxury. Warmth was a dream. And fire? Fire did not exist.
Among the many shivering, clueless Homo erectus who roamed this brutal world, Auauauau was perhaps the most hopeless. He belonged to the Stone River Tribe, a small group of hairy bipeds who spent their days searching for edible roots, running from things with claws, and shaking their fists at the sky when it rained.
But the biggest danger wasn't the wild animals. It was their own kind.
The tribe was ruled by the Strong Ones—a handful of huge, merciless brutes who took what they wanted and crushed anyone who resisted. They controlled the best food, the warmest caves, and most of all, they decided the fate of the tribe’s women. Who could form bonds, who could share shelter, and who was left behind—it was all dictated by their dominance. Those they deemed worthy lived in comfort. Those they ignored remained nameless, unseen, forgotten.
Because strength was everything, the Strong Ones claimed the most admired partners for themselves. They held feasts, led the hunts, and made the rules. Others had to accept their place or face exile—or worse.
And so, Uauauaua—the most beautiful woman of the tribe, with her bright eyes and quick laughter—was expected to choose Ouououou, the strongest and most arrogant of them all. He had the biggest bones, the sharpest sticks, and the most dangerous grin. He and the other Strong Ones held all the advantages, leaving the weaker males to watch from the edges, knowing they would never be given a chance.
Auauauau was one of the Forgotten Ones—the men who had no status, no claim, no future. He spent his days gathering scraps, avoiding beatings, and dreaming of a life where he could be more than just a shadow in the dirt.
Every night, he watched from afar as the Strong Ones laughed, feasted, and took whatever they wanted. And every night, his heart ached—not just for Uauauaua, but for all of them, for every member of the tribe who lived in fear of the few.
He hated them. He hated their power. But what could he do? He was nothing.
And so, every night, Auauauau sat alone, gazing up at the stars—millions of them, scattered across the endless dark sky. They reminded him of Uauauaua’s eyes when she smiled, bright and full of life. He admired them for hours, lost in their glow, just as he had been lost in her.
But no matter how long he stared, the stars never came down to him. They stayed far away, untouchable—just like her.
But the stars would not come down. They only shimmered, distant and untouchable, as if mocking him.
As the cold wind howled through the trees and the dark sky stretched endlessly above, Auauauau’s chest tightened. His fists clenched. His jaw locked. He had spent too many nights watching, waiting, longing.
Something had to change.


################ (Call to Adventure) ################
That morning, Auauauau ran.
He had been chasing the small creature for hours, his legs burning, his stomach empty, his breath ragged. The animal darted through the trees, twisting and turning, but he did not stop. He couldn't. He hadn’t eaten in two days.
Then—he caught it.
His fingers closed around its fragile body, its tiny heart racing against his palm. For a moment, he just stared at it, unable to believe it. Food. His food. His victory.
He exhaled, clutching it tightly, turning toward his cave. For once, he had something. A meal. A small win.
And then—a shadow fell over him.
Ouououou.
Bigger. Stronger. Smirking.
Before Auauauau could react, a heavy fist crashed into his ribs.
Air ripped from his lungs. His body folded in on itself, pain flooding his stomach. His fingers loosened. His grip collapsed.
Ouououou’s hand shot forward, grabbing the struggling animal from Auauauau’s weakened grasp as he stumbled to the ground.
The world spun. Dust filled his mouth. The sound of the tiny creature's desperate kicks faded as Ouououou walked away, chewing on the victory that had never been his.
He didn’t even look back.
It wasn’t a fight. It wasn’t even theft.
It was nothing.
The others saw. They always saw. The Strong Ones laughed, shaking their heads. The rest looked away, pretending not to notice.
Only Uauauaua didn’t.
Her eyes lingered on him—not with laughter, not with pity, but with something else. Sadness. Helplessness. A silent mercy. But like the others, she did nothing. She couldn't.
And that was worse than all of it.
Auauauau lay in the dirt, his empty hands trembling. His stomach screamed. His ribs ached. But worse than all of it was the nothingness.
Slowly, he pushed himself up, legs dragging through the dirt, stepping further and further from the tribe’s gathering place. Away from the laughter. Away from their voices.
As he moved, he didn’t look at the Strong Ones. He didn’t look at the others.
He only looked at Uauauaua’s eyes.
For a moment, just before the night swallowed him, his gaze met hers, filled with something wordless, something raw. Not sorrow. Not anger.
Gratitude.
And then, he was gone.
Alone.
Where he always belonged.

################ (Refusal of the Call) ################
Auauauau walked until his legs ached, until his breath came in heavy, ragged pulls. The night swallowed him whole, the wind cutting through his thin skin, reminding him of what he already knew—he was nothing. He would always be nothing.
His stomach growled angrily, but he ignored it. Hunger was nothing new. Defeat, though—that was something different.
He had lost many things before—scraps of food, sleeping spots, dignity—but this was worse. Ouououou hadn’t just taken his meal. He had reminded everyone, reminded him, of the unspoken law of their world: the strong take, the weak endure.
For a fleeting moment, when Uauauaua had looked at him, something inside had burned. It had whispered to him: No more.
But the night was cold. The world was cruel. And he was still just Auauauau.
He should go back. Back to his cave. Back to the tribe. Back to the world where he belonged—on the edges, ignored, forgotten.
He should accept his place.
But his feet did not turn back.
Instead, they carried him toward the river.
It was a place of solitude, a place where even the Strong Ones did not often go. The river cut through the land like a jagged scar, deep and endless, its waters fierce, its currents unpredictable. Many had gone in. Not all had come back.
Auauauau stood at the edge, staring down at the swirling darkness below.
The cold bit at his skin. His ribs ached. His hands clenched into fists.
And then, suddenly—he roared.
A desperate, furious, hopeless sound, torn from the depths of his chest. A sound meant for no one, meant for everything. He hated this world. Hated the Strong Ones. Hated the way things were.
But most of all, he hated himself.
Because he could not change anything.
Because he would not change anything.
His fists trembled. His chest burned. He had nothing left. No food. No pride. No strength.
So what was he doing out here?
What did he think would happen?
That the stars would finally listen?
That the gods—if they even existed—would care?
For a long time, Auauauau stood at the river’s edge, staring into the abyss below, listening to the water’s endless whisper.
Then, slowly, he turned away.
He would go back. He had to.
There was no other choice.
But just as he took a step toward the world he had always known—
Something flickered.
A movement, a shadow—no, something more. A shimmer beneath the water.
A fish.
His breath caught. His body went still.
He waded into the river, his steps careful, his hands steady despite the hunger gnawing at his insides.
The fish darted left. He lunged.
It slipped through his fingers.
He growled under his breath, adjusting, focusing.
Again.
The water splashed. The fish vanished.
Again.
His fingers brushed against scales, but the river stole them from him.
Again.
Again.
Again.
The failure piled upon him, pressing down on his chest, filling his lungs with something heavier than air.
And then—
Another shimmer. Right below him.
His fingers shot forward, wrapping around it.
His chest tightened. He had it. He finally had it.
He lifted it, his stomach tightening in anticipation, his heart beating faster—
But it wasn’t a fish.
It was just a rock.
A smooth, curved, shiny rock.
For a moment, he simply stared at it.
Then, everything broke.
His chest heaved. His fingers tightened around the useless, stupid, worthless thing in his hands.
The moonlight hit it, catching its surface, making it glow in the dark.
And suddenly, he wasn’t looking at a rock anymore.
He was looking at Uauauaua.
Her eyes in the firelight, lingering on him that night.
Not with laughter. Not with pity.
With sadness. Helplessness. A silent mercy.
And then—he remembered Ouououou.
The fist to his ribs.
The laughter of the Strong Ones.
The sound of his own body hitting the dirt.
His fingers tightened. His jaw clenched.
His stomach ached. His body trembled.
And before he even knew what he was doing—
He threw it. Hard. With everything he had.
The rock crashed against another stone on the riverbank.
A spark.
A tiny burst of light.
Gone in an instant.
But he saw it.


################ (Meeting the Mentor) ################
Auauauau didn’t remember falling asleep.
His legs had carried him far, past the river, past the trees, into a quiet hollow between the rocks. His body had collapsed before his mind could argue. The exhaustion swallowed him whole.
The hunger was still there, gnawing at his insides, but he was too empty to care. Too tired to think.
And then— the dream came.
He was back in his cave.
The one he had left behind the day before. The one that no longer belonged to him.
But something was different.
The darkness inside was heavy, deeper than before, stretching beyond the walls, swallowing the world outside. It felt endless. Ancient.
His fingers clenched around the stone. The shiny rock.
The same weight. The same fury.
He threw it.
The spark came.
But this time, it did not vanish.
It hovered in the air, trembling, flickering—alive.
The ember stretched, curled, breathed. It did not die. It grew.
It spread—rising, unfolding, stretching toward the sky.
And then—
it became the stars.
Millions of tiny, flickering lights, scattered across the darkness like the night sky.
But they did not stay small.
The flickers merged, pulled toward each other, growing brighter, hotter.
The cave walls glowed, pulsing with golden light.
The stars became one.
And suddenly, it was no longer the night.
It was the sun.
Blinding. Burning. Alive.
The warmth touched his skin, seeped into his bones, filled the emptiness inside him. He had never felt this before—not from the weak autumn sun, not from the body heat of the tribe, not from anything in this world.
This was different.
It wanted to live.
It wanted him to see.
The fire curled around him, shifting, breathing. Not just light, not just heat. Something more.
He looked down at his hands. The shiny rock was still there.
But now, it glowed.
The fire curled around it, holding it, feeding from it.
He reached out, fingers trembling.
The glow wrapped around him, whispering without words.
And then—
He woke.
The cold air hit him first.
But something was different.
A weak, golden light stretched across his body—the first touch of the morning sun, peeking over the horizon.
It was real.
Not just a dream.
Not just a vision.
The sun had touched him. The warmth had stayed.
His stomach twisted.
He needed food.
So he rose, brushed dirt from his skin, and set off in search of something—anything—to eat.

The day passed like any other.
He found a few roots. Some bitter berries. Enough to survive, not enough to live.
By the time night came, he sat near the river, chewing slowly, staring into nothing.
And then— the stars appeared.
Millions of them. Flickering. Watching.
His breath caught.
A flash. A memory.

The stones.
And there—the shiny rock.
It caught the faint starlight, glimmering against the dark earth.

He had no reason to.

################ (Crossing the Threshold)
The river was waiting.
Auauauau’s feet ached, his stomach twisted in hunger, and his head pulmed with exhaustion, but still—he kept moving. His body had nowhere else to go, and his mind had only one place to return to.
The shiny rock.
Something about it had called to him that night. The way it caught the moonlight, the way it sparked against the other stone— it had been more than just a rock.
It had been magic.
And he needed it again.
By the time he reached the river, the sun was at its highest point in the sky. The waters shimmered. The stones were warm beneath his fingers. He searched, crawled, pushed through the dirt, overturning rocks, digging with his bare hands.
And then—
His breath caught.
There.
Lying in the sand, half-buried, was his rock.
But it was no longer whole.
It had split in two.
For a moment, he just stared. The memory of that night came rushing back—the anger, the throw, the moment when everything broke. He picked up both halves carefully, turning them in his hands.
They weren’t perfectly round anymore. They weren’t smooth. But they still shimmered.
They were still his.
He exhaled. Then, gripping them tight, he lifted them high into the air—like a trophy, like a prize.
The first step was complete.
Now, he just had to bring back the magic.
(The Struggle: Sparks in the Daylight)
Auauauau sat beside the river, the two halves of his precious rock in hand.
He took a deep breath—and slammed them together.
Nothing.
Again. Harder.
Tap. Clack. Nothing.
His jaw clenched. He adjusted his grip, trying to hold them exactly as he had that night. Another hit. Another. Faster. Harder.
A tiny flicker—then gone.
His heart leapt, but the spark was so faint.
He hit the stones again. Another flicker—but weaker.
The sunlight swallowed it whole.
His breath caught. Something was wrong. The sparks were there, but they weren’t the same. They weren’t bright. They weren’t alive.
He kept trying. Again. Again. Again.
His hands began to shake. His fingers burned. Frustration crept into his bones.
Had he imagined it? Had it all been just a trick of the night?
No. No, no, no.
It was real. It had to be real.
But the day refused to listen. The light from the sky was too strong, too unforgiving. He couldn’t see what was there.
And so—he kept going.
He would wait. He would try until night came.

The sky burned orange. The river darkened. The sun faded behind the trees.
And finally—
The stars returned.
Auauauau’s hands trembled. His heartbeat quickened. The same feeling from that night washed over him.
This was the moment.
He slammed the rocks together again.
BOOM. SPARK.
His eyes widened. It was back. The magic was back.
Again. Spark.
Again. Brighter.
Again. Larger.
The sparks danced before his eyes, just like before. The memory of that first night flooded back—Uauauaua’s eyes, the stars above, the dream of warmth, the dream of something bigger.
His body couldn’t contain it.
He jumped up. His feet slammed against the ground. His arms flung into the air. He spun in wild, erratic circles, throwing the shiny rocks together again and again, covering the night with light.
The first joy dance in history.
His laugh echoed through the trees. He kicked, spun, leaped— his whole body alive in a way it had never been before.
This was his power. His secret.
For the first time since leaving the tribe, he laughed.
Loud. Wild. Free.
The stars above watched. The river whispered.
And Auauauau danced—because he had won.
But the night was not done with him yet.

The night stretched endlessly.
Auauauau sat near the river, the cold biting at his skin, his stomach still empty but his mind ablaze. His hands gripped his shiny rocks, the two pieces of magic that had shown him something no one in his tribe had ever seen. The stars above watched, silent witnesses to his discovery.
He had made sparks.
But sparks were not enough.
They came and went, bright for a moment, then gone. Like Uauauaua’s glance before she looked away. Like the stars that burned above but never touched the earth.
He needed more.
He had spent hours slamming the rocks together, again and again, watching the tiny bursts of light dance before his eyes. But the sparks never stayed. They never became what he had seen in his dream.
And so, he sat, defeated but determined, absentmindedly tapping the rocks together as he thought.
Tap. Clack. Spark.
Tap. Clack. Spark.
His mind drifted. His thoughts wandered. To Uauauaua. To the tribe. To the way Ouououou had taken everything from him.
Tap. Clack. Spark.
And then—
Something smelled different.
A sharp, acrid scent cut through the cold night air.
He blinked.
Something was wrong.
His nostrils flared. His body tensed. He looked down—
And froze.
A small, dark patch was curling on the edge of the fur around his waist.
For a moment, he didn’t understand. His breath slowed. His mind tried to grasp what he was seeing.
Then—
It clicked.
The spark had touched it.
And the fur had changed.
It wasn’t just burned—it had tried to catch fire.
His whole body shivered—not from the cold, but from something else.
Excitement. Fear.
Realization.
Fire was not just a light. Not just magic.
It was alive.
It needed to be fed. Nurtured.
If he could control it, if he could keep it alive—
He would never be weak again.
He gently ran his fingers over the burned patch of fur, watching as the edges crumbled into nothingness.
For the first time in his life, he had found something more powerful than the Strong Ones. More powerful than Ouououou.
Something even the stars above could not do.
He could burn the dark.
His grip on the shiny rocks tightened.
The cold still surrounded him. His stomach still ached. His body still trembled.
But for the first time in history—
A man was no longer powerless against the night.
He would master fire.


################ (Tests, Allies, Enemies) ################

The first thing Auauauau learned about fire was that it hated him.

Not in a passive, indifferent way. Not the way the river was cold or the wind was sharp. No—fire had opinions. Strong ones. And its opinion of Auauauau, at least in those early days, was deeply unfavorable.

He had found his shiny rocks. He had the sparks. He had the burning certainty that something great was about to happen.

What he did not have was any idea what he was doing.

The first morning, he sat beside a pile of rocks and slammed his stones together. The sparks came—bright, electric, beautiful. They fell into the dirt.

And died.

He tried again. Sparks into the dirt. Dead.
Again. Sparks onto a large leaf. Dead.
Again. Sparks onto the back of his hand.

Very, very much alive.

He screamed. He shook his hand. He ran in a small circle. The sparks died on contact, but the skin remained convinced of the injustice.

He sat back down. His hand throbbed. His pride throbbed worse.

He was beginning to suspect that the dream had lied to him.

But then—a memory. The night it had all started. The fur around his waist. The spark had touched it and it had tried to live. Hadn't died immediately. Had wanted to continue.

He looked down at the ground. At the dry grass at the base of the nearest tree. At the curled dead leaves sitting in a pile nearby, left over from autumn.

His eyes narrowed.

He gathered them slowly, carefully, like they were precious. They were. He just didn't know it yet. He piled them into a small nest, the way a bird would build one—dense in the middle and loose around the edges. His fingers worked without knowing why they worked this way. Just a feeling. A pull. A whisper from the dream.

He held the stones above the nest.

Breathed.

Struck.

The sparks fell. And one—just one—landed in the heart of the nest.

For one terrible, wonderful moment, nothing happened.

Then the smoke came. A tiny, delicate curl of it, rising from the center of the dry grass like a question. Like a whisper. Like: Are you ready?

Auauauau's entire body went rigid.

He did not breathe. He did not move. He barely existed.

The smoke grew thicker.

And then—a small, orange flame burst to life, no bigger than his thumb. It flickered, unsteady, uncertain, as if it too was surprised to find itself there.

He stared at it.

It stared back.

He exhaled—too hard. The flame lurched sideways, leaned desperately against the wind, nearly died.

His heart stopped.

But the flame held. Just barely. Clinging to a single leaf, feeding, surviving.

He spent the next three days learning how to breathe near fire. This was harder than it sounds.

(The Tests Continue)
Fire, Auauauau discovered, was a living thing with exactly one personality trait: greed. It wanted more. Always more. Feed it nothing and it died. Feed it everything and it burned down a tree. His first real fire consumed an entire patch of grass and sent a family of rabbits fleeing for their lives while he slapped at the spreading edge with a leafy branch, coughing and cursing in the only language he knew.

He burned his fingers seventeen times. He counted. He didn't know what seventeen was. He still counted.

He burned his fur wrap twice. Both times he had to sit, shivering and furious, waiting for the fire to give him back his dignity.

He burned his left eyebrow completely off. He did not notice for two days.

But each failure taught him something. He learned that small was better than big, at first. He learned that wind was an enemy. He learned that certain woods burned longer, others faster, others not at all. He learned that a ring of stones changed everything—the fire inside a circle of rocks behaved like a tamed thing rather than a wild one.

And then one night, a small bird fell from the tree above him, landing directly in the fire with a sound that made him lurch backward in shock. He stood over it for a long time, certain it was an omen of some kind.

Then the smell reached him.

He had never smelled anything like it in his life. He ate it. He sat very still afterward, staring into the fire with an expression no human being had ever worn before—the specific face of someone who has just understood that everything is about to be different.

Slowly, through trial and scorched pride, he had arrived at a truth: fire did not love you. But it would work with you, if you respected it.

(The Ally Arrives)
It was on the fifth night that the wolf appeared.

It was not yet a wolf in the way that mattered. Currently, it was a gaunt, yellow-furred creature with patchy fur, ribs visible through its sides, and an expression that said: I know this is beneath me, but I'm also very hungry.

It sat at the edge of the firelight, just beyond where the warmth ended, and watched him.

Auauauau stiffened. Every instinct screamed. Wolf. Danger. Run or die.

But the wolf did not move.

It just sat there. Looking at the fire. Looking at him. Looking at the fire again.

He grabbed a stick. The wolf watched him grab the stick. Did not move.

He threw a small rock at it. The rock landed near its paw. It looked at the rock. Looked at him. Did not move.

They stared at each other for a very long time.

Then the wolf lay down, curled its nose under its tail, and went to sleep.

Auauauau did not sleep that night. He sat rigid until dawn, gripping his stick, watching the wolf breathe.

The next night, it came back. Closer this time. And the night after that, closer still.

On the eighth night, Auauauau dropped a piece of his roasted bird in the wolf's direction by accident. The wolf ate it instantly. Then looked at him with an expression that contained, for the very first time in the history of wolves and men, something that might generously be called gratitude.

Auauauau stared.

Then he tore off another piece and dropped it deliberately.

He didn't know what he'd just started. But he felt it—in the way the wolf's tail moved, just once, just slightly. Something had shifted in the world.

(The Second Ally)
He smelled the other one before he saw him.

A sour, fearful smell—familiar. The smell of the tribe. Of the Forgotten Ones.

Auauauau spun around, his rocks in hand, his fire burning low behind him.

A thin man stood between the trees. Hollowed-looking, with wide, terrified eyes and matted hair. He was maybe a few years older than Auauauau, carrying the particular expression of someone who had been caught doing something shameful. He had been watching for a while. That much was clear.

"Oooouua?" Auauauau said. Who are you?

The thin man flinched.

"Oooouua," he said—quieter. Less aggressive.

The thin man was called Iiiiieie. He was, like Auauauau, a Forgotten One—cast further from the tribe than most. He had survived by stealing scraps and hiding near the edges of things. He was, by his own assessment, the least impressive person in the world.

But he had seen the fire.

He had seen it three nights ago, from a distance, and he had been circling ever since—terrified to approach, unable to stay away. He had thought it was a sign from the sky. A god. A monster. Something that would kill him.

It had not occurred to him that it might be Auauauau.

Auauauau, of all people.

Iiiiieie crept forward. Stared at the fire. Looked at Auauauau. Back at the fire. Then, without a word, he sat down beside it.

He held out his hands to the warmth.

And something in his face—the tightness, the haunted look, the constant readiness to run—went slack.

Just for a moment.

Auauauau watched him.

Then he fed the fire more wood.

(The Enemy Returns)
It was Iiiiieie who saw them first.

Not their smoke—their fire was small, controlled. There was smoke from the direction of the tribe. And there were figures moving through the trees with the heavy, unhurried confidence of people who had never needed to be afraid.

Three Strong Ones.

Auauauau's stomach dropped. The wolf was immediately alert, low to the ground, ears flat.

They had come because of the fire. He knew this without needing to think about it. A light in the forest, night after night—they had seen it. And the Strong Ones could not ignore anything they did not control.

"Hide," he said to Iiiiieie—or rather, he made the sounds and gestures that meant this. It was enough.

Iiiiieie was already gone.

Auauauau stayed.

He didn't know why. His body told him to run. Every version of him that had ever existed—every hungry, beaten, forgotten version—told him to disappear into the dark, the way he always had.

But he stayed.

He fed the fire.

The Strong Ones stepped into the clearing. They were enormous. They always looked enormous in the firelight—shadow and muscle, the easy power of men who had never been told no. The one at the front was not Ouououou. But he carried the same expression. The one that said: What is this? And also: It belongs to me now.

They saw the fire.

They stopped.

For a long moment, nobody moved.

The Strong One in front took a step forward. Then another.

And then—the wolf moved.

It rose from behind Auauauau, placed itself between him and the three men, and produced a sound so low and so full of ancient threat that the air itself seemed to reconsider.

The Strong Ones stopped.

The wolf held its position. Did not move. Did not break eye contact.

The Strong One in front looked at the wolf. Looked at the fire. Looked at Auauauau standing behind both of them—ragged, empty-handed, with an expression he couldn't quite read.

And for the first time in Auauauau's life, something shifted in a Strong One's face.

Not fear, exactly.

Uncertainty.

They stood there in the flickering edge of the firelight, the line between warmth and darkness drawn clearly between them. The Strong Ones were in the dark. Auauauau was in the light.

They stayed long enough to see that he was not moving. That the wolf was not moving. That the fire burned steadily, quietly, as though it too was watching them.

Then, without a word, they turned.

And walked back into the trees.

Auauauau watched them go.

His hands were shaking. His chest was heaving. Every part of him had expected to lose—had always expected to lose.

But he hadn't.

He hadn't won, either. Not yet.

But for the first time in his life, the Strong Ones had been the ones to walk away.

He sat back down beside the fire. After a moment, Iiiiieie emerged from behind a tree, looking like a man who had survived something he didn't fully understand. The wolf lay back down and resumed sleeping, as if it had merely interrupted a nap.

Auauauau stared into the fire.

It breathed.

He breathed.

And somewhere in the dark behind them, the three Strong Ones were walking home with something new in their chests—something small and unfamiliar and deeply uncomfortable.

The first human beings to feel afraid of what they couldn't take.
