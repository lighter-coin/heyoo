## 2. The Problem We Solve
2.1 Lighters disappear. Always. Everywhere.
75% of cheap lighters are lost before they run out of fuel. In Turkey alone — with 31% adult
smoking rate — tens of millions of lighters vanish every year. Nobody cared, because they cost
₺5. They were designed to be forgotten. This is W owowowowo's greatest victory: making fire
disposable.

2.2 Location apps die at the cold start
Heyoo is a location-based discovery and territory game built on TON/Telegram. Its core
mechanic requires 500–1,000 active users per neighborhood to feel alive. Without that density,
the map is dark and empty. Traditional user acquisition for location apps costs $10–15 per user.
There is no shortcut — except a physical object already in people's hands.

2.3 Crypto has no physical hook

Every successful TON meme coin (DOGS, Notcoin, Hamster Kombat) collapsed 90–99% after
launch because tokens had no sustained real-world utility. Nothing kept users engaged. No
physical reminder. No reason to return. The token needed a body.

## The Core Insight
A lighter in someone's pocket is a persistent, real-world node. When it disappears, it doesn't

## 4. The Map — Heyoo Hexagon Territory System
4.1 How the Hexagon Grid Works
Heyoo's map is divided into hexagons using Uber's H3 geospatial library — the same system
used by Uber, Airbnb, and major GIS platforms worldwide. Hexagons nest perfectly: each
resolution level contains exactly 7 hexagons of the next finer level.

Resolution 12 (finest) ~1 house / small building footprint — the smallest
claimable unit
Resolution 11 ~7 res-12 hexagons — a city block
Resolution 10 ~49 res-12 hexagons — a neighborhood block
Resolution 9 and above Districts, cities, regions — up to global scale

## 4.2 Ownership Rules
- The first person to open (discover) a Resolution 12 hexagon becomes its permanent
owner.
- Ownership of higher-resolution hexagons is determined by proportional share. Example:
if you own 4 of the 7 res-12 hexagons inside a res-11 hexagon, you hold majority stake
in that res-11 territory.
- Every time someone opens any hexagon, they automatically receive proportional shares
in all parent hexagons above it — from res-12 all the way up.
- This creates a natural incentive: early explorers who discover fine-grained areas gain
territory stake at every scale.

4.3 The Mystery Lighter Placement — Fatih as the First Explorer
The founding explorer (Fatih) will personally travel to new locations, open hexagons on the
Heyoo map, and leave lighters as mystery objects — tagged to that location on the map but
deliberately placed at Resolution 12 hexagons that have NOT been opened yet.


## The Discovery Mechanic
A lighter is tagged to a specific unmapped location. The map shows a marker — a sign that
something is there — but the hexagon remains dark (unclaimed). Only a user who physically
travels to that exact spot, finds the lighter, and scans it can claim and open that Resolution
12 hexagon. The lighter is the key. The territory is the reward.

- Some lighters are deliberately left in public spaces (cafes, parks, streets) —
Wowowowowo-style organic loss. Anyone who finds and scans claims that location.
- Some lighters are placed intentionally as 'mystery deposits' — tagged on the map as a
known-unknown. The hex shows a flame icon but stays dark until someone physically
retrieves the lighter and scans.
- The map creates a Pokémon GO effect: users can see that something exists at a
location without knowing exactly what or where. Curiosity drives physical movement.
